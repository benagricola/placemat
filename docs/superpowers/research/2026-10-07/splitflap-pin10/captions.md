# U7 pin 10 on splitflap_driver: captions

**passing.png** - The board routed by the fork head (78a16663) with upstream 39dd0327's stub-proximity change undone, order seed 0, cropped to about 11 x 10 mm around U7 pin 10 (dashed white ring). Red is top copper, blue is bottom copper, as in KiCad. The green overlay is the +3V3 route that joins pin 10: a short stub to a via just below-right of the pad, east on the bottom layer, and back to the top by a second via. Look at the space under and to the right of the pad: here it is free, because the router ripped MOTOR_F_PHASE_A (which ran under the pad on the bottom layer) and re-routed it above the pin, and CLOCK_IN's tap reaches pin 11 from the upper right.

**failing.png** - The same window from the fork head itself, same seed; pin 10 is left open. The orange area is everywhere a 0.3 mm +3V3 track can reach from pin 10 at 0.25 mm clearance on this saved board, vias included (solid on top, dashed on bottom). It is closed, 4.7 mm2, with no other +3V3 copper in it. This is my geometric fill of the final board, not the router's own map; the earlier trace measured a 373-cell pocket on the router's map during the run. The labelled nets form the wall: MOTOR_E_PHASE_C above, MOTOR_OUT_PRE_BUFFER and CLOCK_IN's via on the left, LATCH_IN below (during the run GND's tap sat there; the rip ladder later replaced it), pin 11 on the right, and on the bottom layer MOTOR_F_PHASE_A under the pad and MOTOR_E_PHASE_D above it. The thin dashed green line is the passing run's +3V3 path; CLOCK_IN's bottom-layer tap crosses it.

**mechanism.png** - Part A says, from the code, what 39dd0327 switches off: a multipoint net stops adding the 2 mm "stay away" cost around its copper's free ends once its Phase 3 taps are done. Part B follows the two runs step by step from their logs: identical through GND's taps; different from the next tap on, because GND's free ends stop costing; CLOCK_IN's tap then reaches U7 pin 11 from the other side; at +3V3's turn pin 10 is boxed in in both runs, but the passing run has one failed tap and rips the blocker under the pad, while the failing run has four, rips other nets and gives up on pin 10. The caption says which parts are facts from the runs and which are mechanism from the code; the step from the dropped costs to CLOCK_IN's different path is a knock-on effect through the rest of the board, not a cost next to pin 10.
